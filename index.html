What it is

Three datasets joined on HubSpot ID: 1,078 webinar registrations across four FDE webinars, 917 analysed sales-call transcripts, and the FDE sales tracker. The registrations tell us who showed up. The tracker tells us who bought. The transcripts — the part that did not exist before — tell us what happened in between, in the customer's own words.

What it is not

Not a forecast. Not a rep scorecard — lead-to-PA assignment for non-converted leads was never supplied, so no PA comparison appears anywhere. Not a model: every figure is counted from the three files, and the only derived numbers are three rupee estimates, each shown with the benchmark used.

The questions this answers
How many did we actually sell, and against what base?
27 total FDE sales = 18 from the webinar funnel + 9 from a different funnel. Of the 18, 15 attended. Two rates: 1.67% registration-to-FDE and 2.48% attendee-to-FDE. Lead Status cannot be used — most leads flagged "Enrolled" last bought in 2022–2025 and are alumni.
Enrolment§2
Is the funnel growing?
Not yet — and it needs attention. FDE conversion has moved 2.28% → 1.48% → 1.81% → 0.89% while registrations moved 307 → 225. Attendance held steady at 55%, so the shift sits after the webinar, not before it.
Numbers§3
Which cohort, channel or domain should we bet on?
No single one. Conversion is compressed between 2.0% and 3.3% across all four experience bands. The clearest signal is negative: 5–8 Talent500 produced 131 leads and zero sales, while 46 non-T500 leads in the same band produced four.
Numbers§3
Where do leads stall, and why?
The CRM says "No Plan" for 37–39% of leads, which is a disposition, not a reason. The transcripts show four named blockers underneath it: timing 185, price 90, role fit 59, and 51 with no category at all. Those are addressable. "No Plan" is not.
Where leads stall§4
Is this a people question or a process question?
Process. Placement objections close at 0% unresolved. Price closes at 6 of 90. Same PAs. The variable is whether a scripted answer exists — so the fix is a document, not a hiring decision. That is good news: it is cheap and fast.
Where leads stall§4
What happens after the first call?
59% of calls (541 of 917) ended with no answer to the objection and no date booked. Enrolled leads received about 3 connects and 40 minutes; the 115 positive-but-stuck leads received 1.5 connects and 18 minutes. The second call was never scheduled.
Where leads stall§4
What did customers actually say?
15 verbatim quotes, customer speech only. Two carry the argument: a lead who said job-switching was his top priority was logged as a timing objection and dropped; a lead asking scholarship criteria went on to buy. Price questions are buying signals, not refusals.
Transcripts§5
How much money is on the table, and where?
₹35.5L across the two largest fixable leaks, against ₹55.9L already realised — with no extra registrations. 5–8 Talent500 ₹23.6L, positive pipeline never getting a second call ₹11.9L, unbooked timing callbacks ₹6.0L. The leaks overlap and must not be summed.
Leakage§6
Is the EdgeUp down-sell acceptable or not?
Mostly acceptable. The rule applied: selling another programme is fine if FDE was tried first and the lead declined. Of the 11 down-sells with call data — 4 OK, 3 flagged (readable call, FDE never raised), 4 need a transcript re-check. Not a policy problem; three PA conversations.
Leakage§6
What do we do on Monday, and who owns it?
Ten actions, each with the number that justifies it. Five cost nothing but a rule change or a document. Two need a budget decision. Owners: Sales 4, Enablement 2, Category 2, Marketing 1, Leadership 1.
Actions§7
Which specific leads do we call back?
93 HIGH priority out of 367 worth keeping — filterable by priority, play and funnel stage, each with the exact sentence the lead said. 85 of the 93 asked a buying question and 76 have an unresolved objection.
367 leads§8
Can I trust these numbers?
Registrations and sales reconcile against your own files. Every section carries a Basis note in the right margin stating sample size and confidence. Where a base is thin — Organic n=44, 20+ n=50, 15 attendee-scope sales — it is labelled directional rather than settled.
Definitions§9
What it cannot answer
  • Which PA or POD performs best. Lead-to-PA assignment for non-converted leads was never supplied. Closer names appear only on won deals, which cannot support a comparison.
  • The CRM status of the 367 leads individually. Status is supplied and used — as percentages by band, and at lead level in the All Sales sheet. But that sheet carries only 36 of 1,058 cohort leads and 13 of the 367. The aggregate picture is answered; the per-lead one is not.
  • What happened on half the calls. Roughly half of all recordings are under 120 seconds or unreadable, and 392 calls gave no interest signal. Conversation percentages describe the readable half.
  • What will happen next. No forecast is made. The rupee figures are current exposure, not projected recovery.
What would unlock more
  • Lead-to-PA assignment for non-converted leads — turns the POD concentration observation into a coaching-or-routing decision.
  • Lead status for the remaining 354 of the 367 — shows which named leads are already filed as lost despite a recorded reason to call back.
  • Per-programme revenue for this window — moves the leak sizes from an average-deal estimate to actual realised value per segment.
Takeaway

Registrations and sales show the start and the end. The transcripts are the only record of the middle — and that is where 18 sales became 18 instead of 35.
